# Compass Rulebook — Phase 0 Spec

**Date:** 2026-06-10
**Status:** Draft for owner approval (Phase 1 may not start until this is approved)
**Audience:** The owner, and any future engineer or agent who picks this up cold.

> **Purpose of this document:** pin down — *before any code is written* — exactly what
> the app claims, how each claim will be tested, what counts as success, and what
> counts as death. Rules are written so that two people implementing them
> independently would produce the same test. Anything left vague here becomes a
> place to fool ourselves later.

---

## 1. Goal and non-goals

### 1.1 Goal

A calm, once-a-day, propose-only advisor that answers three questions at the
**months** timescale:

1. **Where are industries moving?** (theme trend)
2. **Is the money real, or just hype?** (financial reality)
3. **Who in a strong theme hasn't been paid yet?** (laggard radar)

The app's default daily answer is expected to be *"nothing to do today."* That is a
feature. Its job on most days is to prevent action, not provoke it.

### 1.2 Permanent non-goals

These are not deferred — they are rejected, with reasons on record:

| Non-goal | Why |
|---|---|
| Options strategies | Three retail earnings-options approaches were tested honestly in prescient (buy-through, short-vol, vol-ramp); all three failed survival after honest costs |
| Real-time / streaming ingestion | The seconds timescale belongs to HFT firms; retail cannot win that race. Nightly batch only |
| Auto-trading / broker write access | Propose-only forever. The human clicks |
| Intraday signals | Same reason as streaming |
| Prediction of single-day moves | No evidence anywhere in the program that this survives costs |

## 2. Governance (binding)

Inherited from prescient and binding on all phases (full text in `AGENTS.md`):

1. **Evidence overrides the feeling.** Nothing ships without surviving the protocol in §5.
2. **Propose-only forever.** No order-placement code exists in this repo, in any phase.
3. **No-lookahead is sacred.** At simulated date *T*, a strategy may use only data
   knowable at *T*: prices through *T*, fundamentals *reported* by *T* (report date +
   1 trading day), theme memberships as dated at *T*. The realized future is visible
   only to the scorer.
4. **One-shot validation.** Develop on the development window, validate exactly once
   on the validation window. No re-validation after tweaks.
5. **No rescue sweeps.** A hypothesis that hits its kill condition is dead. Parameter
   sweeps exist to test *robustness* (§5.5), never to resurrect a failure.
6. **Free data first.** Paid data must prove its value in a backtest before recurring spend.

## 3. The three hypotheses

Each hypothesis is pinned with: exact rule (named parameters), instruments, success
bar, kill condition, and honest baseline.

Shared cost model (applies to every simulated trade, deliberately pessimistic):

- **Trading cost:** 0.10% of notional per side (slippage + spread; commissions are $0
  at modern brokers, the 0.10% absorbs market impact on liquid names).
- **Tax haircut:** positive P&L on positions held < 365 calendar days is haircut by
  **35%** (short-term capital gains at a high marginal rate). Positions held ≥ 365
  days are haircut by **20%**. Losses are not credited with a tax benefit
  (pessimistic by design).
- **Cash return:** un-invested capital earns the 3-month T-bill rate (proxy: BIL
  total return; if unavailable, 0% — pessimistic).

Shared sample-size honesty labels (identical to prescient):
`too_few` < 30 - `suggestive` 30–149 - `moderate` 150–499 - `strong` ≥ 500.

Shared price convention: **all** prices in signals and P&L are split- and
dividend-adjusted (total-return) daily closes. No intraday data exists anywhere in
this project.

### 3.1 H1 — Theme trend ("is the industry moving?")

**Claim:** holding a theme basket only while it is above its long-term trend line,
and stepping aside when below, beats buying-and-holding the total market after costs
and taxes — primarily by avoiding deep drawdowns.

**Signal definition:**

- `theme_index(T)` = equal-weighted total-return index of the theme's members as of
  date *T* (point-in-time membership, §4), rebalanced monthly.
- For long-lived sectors with a liquid ETF covering the whole development window
  (SMH, XLE, XLF, XLV, JETS, IGV, CIBR — see Appendix A), the **primary test
  instrument is the ETF itself** — its membership evolved naturally over time, which
  removes hindsight bias from the test. In ETF tests, both the signal
  (`close vs SMA(N)`) and the held instrument are the ETF's adjusted close. ETFs
  listed after 2006 (JETS 2015, CIBR 2015) are tested from listing + 250 trading
  days (enough history to compute the SMA). Hand-curated baskets are the secondary
  test.

**Rule (parameters in brackets):**

- Evaluate on the **last trading day of each month** [evaluation frequency: monthly].
- If `theme_index close > SMA(N)` with [N = 200 trading days]: hold the theme
  instrument for the next month.
- Else: hold cash (T-bill proxy) for the next month.
- Execution: at the close of the first trading day of the next month (signals never
  trade on the bar that produced them).

**Success bar (all required):**

1. Sharpe ratio (monthly returns, annualized) > buy-and-hold of the same instrument, after costs and tax haircut.
2. Max drawdown at least 25% smaller (relative) than buy-and-hold's max drawdown.
3. CAGR after costs/taxes ≥ buy-and-hold CAGR − 1.0 percentage point (the rule may
   give up a little return to buy its drawdown protection, but not more than 1 point).
4. Holds on the validation window (§5.3), not just development.

**Kill condition (any one):** fails bar 1 or 2 on the development window; fails
catastrophically on validation (Sharpe below buy-and-hold AND drawdown not improved);
or dies inside the sensitivity band (§5.5) — i.e., works at N=200 but fails at
N=175 or N=225.

**Baseline:** buy-and-hold of the same instrument, and buy-and-hold of the total
market (VTI), both charged the same cost model.

### 3.2 H2 — Financial reality ("is the money real?")

**Claim:** themes whose members show real, accelerating fundamentals outperform
themes that are price-hype only, over the following 3–6 months.

**Score definition (computed quarterly, per theme, as-of date *T*):**

For each member with a report dated ≤ *T* − 1 trading day, using its latest reported
quarter:

- `rev_growth` = YoY revenue growth (%)
- `margin_delta` = YoY change in operating margin (percentage points)
- `surprise` = last EPS surprise (%, actual vs estimate)

`member_score` = mean of the three values' **cross-sectional z-scores** (each metric
standardized across all universe members that quarter; missing metric → that z-score
omitted from the mean; member with all three missing → excluded).
`theme_score(T)` = median of its members' `member_score`.

**Test (this is a ranking test, not a trading rule):**

- At each calendar quarter-end *T*: rank all themes by `theme_score(T)` into
  terciles (top / middle / bottom). With the 10-theme seed universe the split is
  3 / 4 / 3; in general, remainders go to the middle tercile.
- Measure each tercile's equal-weighted forward return over the next [horizon: 63
  and 126 trading days], using the theme baskets, charged entry+exit costs.

**Success bar (all required):**

1. Top tercile beats bottom tercile by ≥ 2.0 percentage points annualized (after costs) on the development window.
2. Monotonic on average: top ≥ middle ≥ bottom.
3. Direction holds (top > bottom) on the validation window.

**Kill condition:** no monotonic relationship on development, or top-minus-bottom
spread ≤ 0 on validation.

**Baseline:** equal-weighted average of all themes (i.e., does ranking add anything
over just owning everything?).

**Role in the app if it survives:** a filter and a displayed score — H2 is never a
standalone trading rule; it gates and annotates H1/H3 recommendations.

### 3.3 H3 — Laggard radar ("who hasn't been paid yet?")

**Claim:** when a theme's leader has run hard and a fundamentally comparable peer
has badly lagged, the laggard catches up often enough to beat holding the theme
basket.

**Definitions (as-of date *T*, within one theme):**

- `R90(m)` = member *m*'s total return over the trailing [lookback: 90 trading days].
- **Leader** = member with the highest `R90`.
- **Trigger:** leader's `R90` ≥ [+30%] AND the theme is in H1 uptrend
  (`theme_index > SMA(200)`) at *T*.
- **Laggard candidate** = any member *m* with:
  - `R90(m)` ≤ leader's `R90` − [gap: 25 percentage points], and
  - fundamentals comparability: *m*'s latest reported YoY revenue growth ≥ theme
    median − [tolerance: 5 percentage points] (reported as of *T*, no peeking), and
  - market cap ≥ [$2B] and 90-day median dollar volume ≥ [$10M] (tradability floor).

**Rule:**

- Scan on the last trading day of each month. Enter each qualifying laggard at the
  next trading day's close. One position per member at a time.
- Exit at the earlier of: [holding period: 126 trading days] elapsed, or the theme's
  H1 trend turning off at a monthly evaluation.
- In Phase 2, each trade is scored as an independent, equal-notional unit (no
  portfolio sizing, no capital constraint) — the question is whether the *signal*
  has an edge; sizing is a Phase 3 concern and only if H3 survives.

**Success bar (all required):**

1. Mean excess return per trade vs holding the **theme basket** over the identical
   window > 0 after costs and tax haircut.
2. Base rate (% of trades where laggard beats the basket) > 50%, and the 95%
   binomial confidence interval excludes 50% — i.e., distinguishable from a coin flip.
3. Sample size ≥ `suggestive` (30 trades) on development; verdict is capped at the
   honesty label of the sample.
4. Direction holds on validation.

**Kill condition:** base rate CI includes 50% on development with ≥ 30 trades, or
mean excess return ≤ 0, or validation flips the sign.

**Baseline:** holding the equal-weighted theme basket over the identical windows
(NOT the market — the question is whether picking the laggard beats just owning the
theme).

**Known honest risk, on record:** laggards often lag *for a reason* (share loss,
inferior product). The fundamentals-comparability filter is the only defense pinned
here; if the base rate shows the filter doesn't separate catch-ups from deserved
laggards, H3 dies and the app ships without it.

## 4. Universe and themes

- The seed universe is **Appendix A**: 10 hand-curated themes, ~70 tickers, with ETF
  proxies where a faithful one exists.
- **Memberships are dated facts.** Every membership row carries `member_since` (and
  `member_until` when applicable). A backtest at simulated date *T* may only see
  memberships where `member_since ≤ T < member_until`.
- **Hindsight-bias caveat (binding):** this universe was written in June 2026 with
  full knowledge of which themes worked. Backdating memberships flatters any
  backtest. Mitigations, in order of strength:
  1. H1's primary verdict comes from **long-lived ETFs** whose composition evolved
     naturally (no hindsight).
  2. Hand-curated basket results are labeled `hindsight-curated` in every scorecard
     and may strengthen, but never solely produce, a `survived = true`.
  3. `member_since` for backdated members must carry a one-line justification (e.g.,
     "MU has been a memory manufacturer since the 1980s" vs "SMCI joined AI servers
     theme 2023-01").
- Themes created after Phase 5 (auto-detection) get `member_since` = detection date,
  eliminating the problem for all future data.

## 5. Backtest protocol (the referee's calibration)

### 5.1 Calibrate the referee first

Before any compass hypothesis is judged, the harness must reproduce a **published**
result: Faber (2007), *A Quantitative Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation* — the
10-month SMA timing rule on the S&P 500. Acceptance: on 1990–2012 monthly data, the
harness's timing model (before tax haircut, to match the paper) must show the
documented signature: CAGR within ±1.5 percentage points of buy-and-hold, and max
drawdown no worse than 60% of buy-and-hold's max drawdown. If it cannot reproduce a
known result, the harness is broken and nothing it says counts.

### 5.2 Data windows

- **Development window:** 2006-01-01 → 2019-12-31 (includes the 2008 crash and the
  2010–2019 bull — both regimes).
- **Validation window:** 2020-01-01 → present (includes the 2020 crash, 2021 mania,
  2022 bear, 2023–2025 AI run).

### 5.3 One-shot validation

All parameter choices are frozen on development data. Each hypothesis touches the
validation window **exactly once**. The result — pass or fail — is final and recorded.

### 5.4 What every scorecard reports

CAGR (after costs and taxes) - Sharpe - max drawdown - median AND mean trade/period
return - number of trades/periods - sample-size honesty label - baseline values
side-by-side - `survived: true/false` with the specific bar(s) passed/failed.

### 5.5 Sensitivity sweeps (robustness, not rescue)

Run AFTER the development verdict, to test fragility — never to find better numbers:

- H1: N ∈ {150, 175, 200, 225, 250}; evaluation frequency weekly vs monthly.
- H2: horizon 63 vs 126 days; mean vs median member aggregation.
- H3: lookback {60, 90, 120}; gap {20, 25, 30} points; hold {63, 126} days.

An effect that exists only at one parameter point is noise and fails its hypothesis.

### 5.6 Verdict record

| Hypothesis | Development | Sensitivity | Validation | Final |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 theme trend | pending | pending | pending | **pending** |
| H2 financial reality | pending | pending | pending | **pending** |
| H3 laggard radar | pending | pending | pending | **pending** |

This table is updated in place as Phase 2 lands verdicts, and mirrored in the README.

carries its real-world date AND `ingested_at`) - FastAPI with versioned `/v1` routes
(Phase 3) - nightly batch jobs only - front end (Phase 4) renders, never computes.

not built. The result is written up (prescient program-review style), and the
project returns to brainstorm with the verdicts as constraints. This outcome costs
~$0 and is a legitimate success of the process.

## 8. Glossary (plain English)

- **Theme / basket:** a group of companies riding the same economic story, held
  together as one position instead of betting on a single name.
- **ETF:** a single fund that holds a whole basket for you — one purchase, instant
  diversification within the theme.
- **Moving average (SMA(N)):** the average closing price over the last N trading
  days — a slow, smooth line that filters out daily noise. Price above the line ≈
  uptrend intact; below ≈ trend broken.
- **Relative strength:** how much something rose *compared to* the market or its
  peers. Up 10% when everything is up 15% is weakness, not strength.
- **Drawdown:** the fall from a peak to the following trough. The number that
  actually makes people panic-sell. A 50% loss needs a 100% gain to get even.
- **CAGR:** compound annual growth rate — the smoothed per-year return.
- **Sharpe ratio:** return earned per unit of stomach-churn (volatility). Higher =
  better reward for the bumps endured.
- **Base rate:** how often something historically worked. "6 of 9 times" is a base
  rate; "strong buy" is marketing.
- **Tercile:** the top, middle, or bottom third of a ranked list.
- **Point-in-time / no-lookahead:** at any simulated past date, using only what was
  knowable then. A backtest that peeks at the future will "prove" anything.
- **Hindsight bias / survivorship:** picking today's known winners and testing them
  on the past — the test inherits the answer. Why dated memberships matter.
- **Overfitting:** tuning rules until they fit history's accidents instead of its
  patterns. Looks brilliant backwards, loses forwards.
- **Buy-and-hold baseline:** the do-nothing competitor every rule must beat. If a
  strategy can't beat napping, the napping wins.
- **Short-term capital gains:** profits on positions held under a year, taxed at the
  full income rate — the cost most backtests forget and this one charges.
